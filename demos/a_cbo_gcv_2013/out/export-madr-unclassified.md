## Considered Options

- **Army's plan: field the Ground Combat Vehicle** (alt-gcv) [evaluated]: The notional GCV — the December 2010 Design Concept After Trades: 1,748 vehicles, 50 to 65 tons, 9 passengers and a crew of 3, average procurement unit cost capped at $13.5 million in 2013 dollars (pp. 2, 7, 8). Programmatic risk: High.
- **Option 1: purchase the Israeli Namer armored personnel carrier** (alt-namer) [evaluated]: 1,748 Namer APCs on the GCV's procurement schedule (pp. 2, 20): 68 to 70 tons, 9 passengers. The best protected vehicle CBO examined (p. 24) and the least lethal — its largest weapon is a 12.7 mm machine gun (p. 25; described as its primary weapon at p. 23). Programmatic risk: Low.
- **Option 2: upgrade the Bradley infantry fighting vehicle** (alt-upgraded-bradley) [evaluated]: 1,748 upgraded Bradley IFVs: 35 to 41 tons, 7 passengers. More lethal than the notional GCV, less mobile, and two passengers short of a full squad (pp. 2, 20). Programmatic risk: Intermediate.
- **Option 3: purchase the German Puma infantry fighting vehicle** (alt-puma) [evaluated]: 2,048 Pumas — five per platoon rather than four, to keep 28 seats in the platoon: 35 to 47 tons, 6 passengers. The most lethal vehicle CBO examined (pp. 2, 20, 24). Programmatic risk: Low.
- **Option 4: retain the current Bradley infantry fighting vehicle** (alt-retain-bradley) [evaluated]: Extend the life of 820 current Bradley IFVs and continue research and development. No new capability; this is the reference vehicle every improvement is measured against — the M2A3 version used in Iraq, with reactive and underbelly armor (pp. 2, 21). Programmatic risk: None.
