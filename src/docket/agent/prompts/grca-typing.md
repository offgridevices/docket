# Ground rules, constraints, assumptions — OAS AoA Handbook §4.9 (printed p. 45)

Source: *Analysis of Alternatives (AoA) Handbook — A Practical Guide to the Analysis of
Alternatives*, Office of Aerospace Studies, HAF/A5RA-OAS, 4 August 2017, §4.9 "Task 7:
Developing the Key Ground Rules, Constraints, and Assumptions", printed p. 45.

Three distinct types. The Handbook warns, verbatim: "Despite their importance, GRC&As are
typically misunderstood, resulting in the tendency to misuse the terms. This misunderstanding
can cause teams to default to labeling most elements as assumptions, when, in fact, it is not
the appropriate term to use." (§4.9, printed p. 45)

- **Ground rule** — verbatim: "broadly stated procedure that governs the general process,
  conduct, and scope of the study." (§4.9, printed p. 45)
  Illustration, not a value — the Handbook's own: "the study sponsor will review and approve
  the description of the baseline capability prior to the study team conducting the analysis."
- **Constraint** — verbatim: "imposed limitation that can be physical, programmatic, or
  policy." (§4.9, printed p. 45)
  Illustration, not a value — paraphrased from the Handbook's three examples on the same page:
  human physical or cognitive limitations, or a specific operating frequency range, are
  physical; the latest acceptable initial operational capability (IOC) date is programmatic; a
  treaty is policy.
- **Assumption** — verbatim: "a supposition that something is true and can address various
  aspects associated with scoping and supporting the analysis." (§4.9, printed p. 45)
  Illustration, not a value — the Handbook's own: "specific manpower levels, inclusion of a
  target type that will proliferate in the future (thus forcing consideration of a specific
  threat system), or a certain infrastructure or architecture will be provided by another
  program."

The Handbook also names the failure mode you are here to prevent, verbatim: "The WIPT should
refrain from assuming something away for various reasons such as the data does not exist, it
is too difficult to analyze, or there is no time or resources to assess it." (§4.9, printed
p. 45) An element left out for one of those reasons is an omission on someone's authority, not
an assumption, and you do not have that authority.

Army CBA Guide v3.3 (printed p. 19) types *fact*, *constraint* and *assumption* separately.
Verbatim: "A fact is something that is empirically true and can be supported by evidence."
Treat a *fact* as a GroundRule with a source.

Annotation rule: type by the definition, not by the writer's word choice. A sentence the
request calls an assumption is a Constraint if it is an imposed limitation, and a GroundRule
if it governs how the study is run. If the request gives no source for a ground rule or
constraint, or no rationale or evidence for an assumption, record a gap on that field.
