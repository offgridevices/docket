# Assumptions — ICD 203 §D.6.e(3) (ODNI, Intelligence Community Directive 203, Analytic Standards, 02 January 2015, p. 3)

Verbatim: "Products should state assumptions explicitly when they serve as the linchpin of an argument or when they bridge key information gaps. Products should explain the implications for judgments if assumptions prove to be incorrect. Products also should, as appropriate, identify indicators that, if detected, would alter judgments."

Same section, the definition: "Assumptions are defined as suppositions used to frame or support an argument; assumptions affect analytic interpretation of underlying intelligence information." (§D.6.e(3), p. 3)

Annotation rule: every Assumption carries `linchpin` (true if the argument fails without it), `implicationsIfWrong`, and `indicatorsThatWouldAlter[]`. If the request gives no rationale or evidence for an assumption, record a gap on that field; never invent a source.

Uncertainty lexicon — §D.6.e(2), p. 3: use one lexicon row only. The percentage ranges are the Directive's own definition of each band and are not a value to record anywhere: almost no chance (01-05%) · very unlikely (05-20%) · unlikely (20-45%) · roughly even chance (45-55%) · likely (55-80%) · very likely (80-95%) · almost certain (95-99%). Never mix the two rows. Verbatim: "Analysts are strongly encouraged not to mix terms from different rows."

The Directive also separates likelihood from confidence, verbatim: products "must not combine a confidence level and a degree of likelihood, which refers to an event or development, in the same sentence." (§D.6.e(2)(b), p. 3) Keep them apart.
